## Additional vocabulary integrity work

The [vocabulary audit](VOCABULARY_AUDIT_2026-09-05.md) documents an ESDB-backed, licensed, reproducible distinction between accepted historical words and fair required puzzle vocabulary. Fresh boards exclude 58 unreviewed forms; ordinary required routes use the reviewed common band, advanced banks admit the stated wider band, and new dictionary entries require positive audited membership. Saved older boards retain their original validity for continuation/restart. Daily board versions prevent changed pools from sharing a ranked cohort.

Bank selection and generated/extended authored routes are checked before delivery. Independent review found six emergency fallback boards no longer solvable after the fresh-dictionary change. Those six authored fallback entries were replaced; all 60 fallback boards now have a fair complete route, cached selection revalidates eligibility, and callers cannot mutate a shared cached array. Historical bank solvability still uses the historical dictionary; fresh fallback tests use current rules. See `puzzleVocabulary.test.ts`, `fallbackVocabulary.test.ts`, `bankSolvability.test.ts` and the [machine-readable vocabulary report](review-2026-09-05/vocabulary-bank-audit.json).

The [fresh bank delivery validation](reports/BANK_DELIVERY_VALIDATION_2026-09-05.md) verifies all 3,809 delivered boards from the 9,611 historical bank records: 1,465 standard boards retain at least two legal routes, 1,057 Reverse boards complete both legs, and 1,287 Double Shift boards complete their paired moves. Canonical stored solutions and preferred Reverse hint continuations were replayed. The runtime qualifier repairs 23 inconsistent Reverse metadata records only after a proved replay; one single-route standard record and five further replay-vocabulary failures are withheld. Generated source banks remain untouched. The smaller eligible pools can repeat sooner, especially advanced Reverse Lexicon; that content-capacity limit still needs a measured top-up decision.

Do not interpret spell-list membership as a player comprehension study. Native generation latency, rare-word demand and real cohort solve time remain measurement gates.
